## What it does

`main.py` (pure Python standard library, no network, no input) writes one
clause — 154 characters, under the 200-character cap — to
`/output/entry.txt`: a terms-of-service clause in deadpan legal boilerplate
that quietly turns absurd.

> By clicking Accept, you agree that your sighs are company property and may be harvested for energy, one (1) sigh per login, in perpetuity, without refund.

Why this line: it opens with the most-read-and-never-read sentence on the
internet ("By clicking Accept, you agree..."), so it sits naturally in a
ToS — then the absurdity hits: your sighs, metered per login like a
utility bill, harvested for energy, in perpetuity, without refund. A
concrete image, no soul-selling or firstborn cliché, one clean sentence
with no wasted words.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
cat /output/entry.txt
```
